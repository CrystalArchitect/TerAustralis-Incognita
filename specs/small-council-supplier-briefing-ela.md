## The Process

**ASA Statement of Engagement** verifies:
1. ELA's launch licensing and operational readiness (already documented in your licensing)
2. Integration capability (can ELA receive and launch Magellan-manufactured components?)
3. Supply chain coordination (has ELA worked with upstream manufacturers? Or would this be new?)

It does **not** require new certifications—it references your existing launch licenses and operational record.

---

## One Critical Question

**Has ELA worked with Magellan or other Australian aerospace manufacturers before?**

This is key to verification:
- **If yes:** ASA confirms existing integration relationships
- **If no:** We verify ELA's integration **capability** (not necessarily that launches have happened, but that integration is technically feasible)

This would be discussed in the week 10 call.

## Questions We Expect You'll Have

**Q: What does "verify integration" mean operationally?**  
A: ASA would review:
- Your launch license and operational procedures
- Coordination protocols with upstream manufacturers (Magellan)
- Technical compatibility (can ELA receive and stage Magellan components for launch?)
- Interface documentation (how do components hand off from Magellan to ELA?)

It's not a full launch test—it's verification that integration is technically sound.

**Q: Is this public?**  
A: Yes. The SoE names ELA as the sovereign launch integrator and partner in the verified supply chain. This is by design—sovereignty verification requires transparency.

**Q: What about proprietary launch operations?**  
A: ASA focuses on high-level integration capability, not launch procedures. Operational security, vehicle design details, customer specifics—those remain confidential. The SoE confirms you can do it, not how you do it.

**Q: What's the timeline for us?**  
A: ~20-25 hours of staff time over 3 months:
- Week 10 conversation: 30-45 minutes
- Week 10-11 Small Council call: 2-3 hours
- Follow-up and coordination: 4-6 hours
- Documentation/audit support: 10-15 hours (weeks 11-12 and into Q1)

**Q: Do we need board approval?**  
A: That's your call. Our timeline allows for it. Exploratory conversation in week 10, formal commitment decision in week 11-12, so you have time for internal vetting.

**Q: What if we've never launched manufactured components from Magellan before?**  
A: That's fine. The question is: can ELA integrate and launch Magellan-manufactured components? Technically, yes. Verification would confirm that capability and compatibility without requiring a prior successful launch.

**Q: Will this affect our existing customer relationships?**  
A: It shouldn't. This is about verifying that Australian components **can** be integrated and launched from ELA. It doesn't change your customer acquisition or launch service model.

**Q: What if we want to stay neutral / not pick sides between Lynas vs. other REE suppliers?**  
A: This SoE is specifically about the Australian supply chain we've documented (Lynas → Magellan → ELA). You're not required to sign exclusive arrangements. You're confirming that you can work with this particular supply chain for verification purposes.

## The Unique Value of Launch Integration

Unlike Lynas (who is a supplier) or Magellan (who is a manufacturer), **ELA's participation is the proof-of-concept.**

When Lynas and Magellan say "yes," they're confirming capability.  
When **ELA says "yes,"** you're saying "this chain actually works end-to-end because we can integrate and launch it."

That's the most powerful verification possible—the final customer in the chain confirming the system is real and operationable.
